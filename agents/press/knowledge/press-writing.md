## What journalists say lands

Two vendor-run surveys, both fielded early 2026, neither with a francophone-Quebec
counterpart — no Quebec body publishes one, so the QC side is the least evidenced.

- **Cision, *State of the Media 2026*** — 1,899 journalists, 19 markets incl.
  Canada and the US, Jan–Feb 2026
  ([primary PDF](https://www.prnewswire.com/content/dam/prnewswire/resources/white-papers/Cision_2026_State_of_the_Media_Report.pdf), read 2026-09-08).
- **Muck Rack, *State of Journalism 2026*** — 897 retained of 1,044, mostly US
  plus UK, Canada, India, 30 Jan–2 Mar 2026. The report page is bot-gated; figures
  marked † were read from Muck Rack's
  [own release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/19/3259178/0/en/muck-rack-s-2026-state-of-journalism-report-finds-82-of-journalists-use-ai.html),
  the rest from write-ups
  ([Durée & Co.](https://dureeandcompany.com/muck-racks-2026-state-of-journalism-report/),
  [The PR Net](https://theprnet.com/news/43828)).

## Anatomy of a pitch

- **First person, the artist's voice** — warm, direct; never the words "pitch",
  "press release" or "publicist" in the body (house rule).
- **Six beats, in order:** hook / angle → who (genre, city, two sonic
  references) → what this release is → credibility, only if real and impressive →
  date, format, listen link → one ask (listen, interview, review).
- **Under 200 words.** [`press-draft-pitch`](../skills/press-draft-pitch/SKILL.md)
  allows 120–250; the top of that range is above what 69% ask for — aim at the
  bottom.
- **The angle is the reader's, not the artist's** — what it means for *their*
  community; for Quebec media the local angle is the story.
- **Ask for something a journalist can use:** an interview beats "let me know
  what you think" (58% / 42% want interview access).
- **A listening link, always; an attachment, never.** No survey measures whether
  a link-less pitch is deleted — the outlets' own pages ("submissions without
  music will not be considered", Exclaim!) do.
- **No greeting, no sign-off in the master pitch** — the composer adds both per
  contact; otherwise the email doubles them.
- **No invented stat, quote, credit or chart position.** A gap is an UPPERCASE
  placeholder — `[RELEASE DATE]`, `[LIEN D'ÉCOUTE]` — never a question back.
- **US press opens with an FFO line** — "Montréal indie-pop artist X, FFO
  A and B, single out DATE" — two or three acts the outlet already covers.

### Adapt per type, from one master

| Contact type | What changes |
|---|---|
| Press, dailies | lead with the story, not "review my album"; a date to hang it on |
| Blogs | the angle plus the FFO line; short |
| TV / video | the performance — a live clip or video link up front |
| Radio, playlists | promotion writes these — different length, different ask |

## The press release

Third-party evidence stops here: no survey measures the music press release
itself (22% of journalists in Cision 2026 want *more* press releases — the
lowest-ranked resource). The shape below is practitioner convention
([Ari's Take, 2014](https://aristake.com/how-to-write-a-press-release-and-get-press/)
where marked; the rest unsourced trade practice).

1. **Headline** — the news, not the band name: what, who, when. It is also the
   email subject (Ari's Take).
2. **Lead** — one sentence: `<Artist> releases <title>, <format>, <date>, via
   <label | independent>`. Who, what, where, when, how much (Ari's Take).
3. **The project** — two or three specific sentences a writer can lift; "a
   haunting blend of shoegaze guitars and R&B vocal runs", not "a unique mix".
   Adjectives yes, superlatives never (Ari's Take).
4. **A quote from the artist**, in first person — the only first-person line.
5. **Bio and credibility** — two sentences, real accolades only.
6. **Details** — release date, formats, listen link, photo download, socials,
   one contact line.

- **Third person, under one page** (Ari's Take) — practitioners put it under
  500 words.
- **French first in Quebec**, English added for the rest of Canada and the US;
  the two are versions, not translations of each other's idioms.
- **Embargo line at the top** — `EMBARGOED UNTIL <date>` or `FOR IMMEDIATE
  RELEASE` (practitioner).
- **In the email body, never attached** — "DO NOT ATTACH IT TO THE EMAIL"
  (Ari's Take); attachments are filtered and unopened.
- **Assets linked, not sent:** hi-res photo (landscape and portrait; 2000 px
  wide is the practitioner floor, no outlet publishes one), private stream,
  video, presskit. Hi-res images are among the elements journalists value most
  (Muck Rack 2026 †).
- **The release is not the pitch.** The pitch is the short personal email; the
  release sits below it or one click away.

## Follow-up

- **One follow-up is what journalists ask for** (64% Cision; "one, timely" Muck
  Rack). The department's pipeline caps `follow_up_count` at two
  ([`data/artist/press-relation-campaign.sql`](../../../data/artist/press-relation-campaign.sql));
  the second is the exception.
- **When:** 5–7 business days after the pitch is the practitioner norm;
  [`press-run-campaign`](../skills/press-run-campaign/SKILL.md) flags a pitch at
  14 days, a house threshold.
- **Shape:** two lines — the song, the date sent, the link again. Nothing new to
  read.
- **When a journalist replies, answer within 24 hours** — 52% expect it (Cision).
- **"One follow-up doubles your response rate" has no source**; the department
  does not say it.

## Register — EN / FR

| | Quebec (FR) | Rest of Canada · US (EN) |
|---|---|---|
| Register | professional Québécois French — « guillemets », *courriel*, *relance*, *vitrine*, inclusive forms; not France French | clear professional English, no regional register |
| Which language | the **contact's** language, never the app's; `bilingual` takes the artist's `preferred_locale` so they can answer the reply | `en` |
| Greeting / sign-off (composer adds) | « Bonjour à l'équipe de {outlet} ! » · « Merci ! {artist} » | "Hello to the {outlet} team!" · "Thank you! {artist}" |
| Placeholders | `[NOM DE L'ARTISTE]` `[DATE DE SORTIE]` `[LIEN D'ÉCOUTE]` `[INSÉRER LIEN]` | `[ARTIST NAME]` `[RELEASE DATE]` `[LISTEN LINK]` `[INSERT LINK]` |
| Subject | `Nouveauté — {artist} — {title}` | `New release — {artist} — {title}` |

Whether francophone outlets flagged `bilingual` truly welcome an English pitch is
unmeasured; the default reads back to the artist's language for that reason.
