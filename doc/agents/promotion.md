# Promotion

**Status:** 🧪 beta

> Gets your record **played** — radio stations and playlists, with the route in and what it costs.

## In a real label

**Who does it.** A radio promoter. Berklee: a liaison between the artist and
radio, hired per campaign, who pitches new tracks to program and music
directors, secures airplay, arranges interviews and tracks chart positions
([Berklee](https://www.berklee.edu/careers/roles/radio-promoter)). Playlists are
the same desk's streaming-era work. At an independent label it is the `radio@`
inbox ([Sub Pop](https://www.subpop.com/contact)). In Quebec the job has a name:
*pisteur radio*.

**Week to week.** Pick the one single most stations can play, deliver it 6–8
weeks before release, call each music director a few days later, then — once
added — call weekly for the spin count. "Le pistage radio consiste en 90 % de
psychologie et 10 % de connaissances"
([Local9, Comment jouer à la radio](https://local9.ca/docs/guide_promotion_radio.pdf)).

**Why Canada is different: the quota.** A Canadian station must play Canadians.
Commercial popular-music stations play at least 35% Canadian content each week;
French-language stations at least 65% French vocal music
([CRTC — Canadian content](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/cancon/r_cdn.htm) ·
[French vocal music](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/cancon/r_french.htm)). A song is
Canadian under **MAPL** — Music, Artist, Performance, Lyrics — when it meets 2
of the 4 ([CRTC, MAPL](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/info_sht/r1.htm)). A
French-language artist has both quotas working for them. The United States has
no equivalent: a US playlist is a commercial decision end to end.

**What it costs.** Delivery is priced; the follow-up is quoted.

| Buys | Price | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery to Canadian campus and community radio | CAD 7 per song · CAD 50 per album | [!earshot Distro](https://earshot-distro.ca/cost/) |
| Delivery to francophone Canadian radio | CAD 200 per title · 300 per EP · 500 per album (2026) | [45tours](https://www.45tours.ca/commentCaMarche) |
| A curator's listen with a reply within 7 days | EUR 1 per credit, 2 credits per standard submission | [Groover](https://groover.co/en/lp/pricing/) |
| A radio promoter's follow-up | varies — no body publishes a rate; ask for a quote | [PURCOM](https://purcom.ca/le-metier-de-pisteur-radio-demystifie/) |

None of these buys a second of airplay or a placement, and every vendor says so
on its own page.

**The line with the next desk.** Promotion gets you *played*; [Press](./press)
gets you *written about*. Apple Music and Deezer editorial pitches go through
your distributor, so "will you forward my pitch?" is a
[Distribution](./distribution) question.

## Here

The Promotion Agent ranks the stations and playlists worth your release, names
how each one is reached and what that route costs, then writes the pitch that
goes through it. Campus and community radio comes before commercial, because
that is the tier an unsigned artist can enter. Open it with `houselabel promotion`.

| Skill | What you get | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Match radio | a shortlist of stations for your country, language and genre — each with its route (direct, !earshot Distro, 45tours, DMDS), its published rules, and the price | beta |
| Match playlists | playlists split by door: the platform's editorial form (before release), an independent curator (release week), or an algorithm that cannot be pitched | beta |
| Draft the pitch | the words for one station or one playlist, in your voice, shaped by that route's limits; opened as a Gmail draft | beta |

**The Spotify editorial window.** You pitch one song per release from inside
Spotify for Artists at least **7 days before** release; the form never reopens,
and pitching early is what puts the song in your followers' Release Radar
([Spotify](https://support.spotify.com/artists/article/pitching-music-to-playlist-editors)).
Apple Music wants 10 days before and the pitch goes through your distributor
([Apple Music Connect](https://musicconnect.apple.com/support/5575-apple-music-pitch));
Amazon opens only after release, up to 14 days
([Amazon Music for Artists](https://artists.amazonmusic.com/pitch)). One
"pitch by" date on a plan is wrong for at least two platforms, so the agent gives
each its own.

**What it never does**

- Submits or sends. The form is yours to fill; the email is a draft you send.
- Sells a guaranteed placement. Paying a curator to listen is legitimate;
  paying for a guaranteed add is how Spotify flags artificial streaming and
  withholds royalties ([Spotify](https://artists.spotify.com/en/artificial-streaming)).
- Invents a station, a curator, a contact or a price. No published price is
  reported as "unpublished", never as a guess.
- Blends the Canadian and US strategies. Your country decides the whole plan.
- Pitches the same music director twice for one record.

**What it needs from you**

- On file already: your country, province, language and genre; the release and
  its date (days-to-release decides which doors are still open); what has been
  pitched before.
- It will ask once: the final WAV and a radio edit if the song runs past 3:30,
  your mailing address for the footer, and which single leads.

**Example**

> **Artist:** We're a bilingual folk duo in Sherbrooke, single out in five weeks. Radio?
> **Agent:** Five weeks is inside the 6–8-week delivery window, so this week: !earshot Distro for the English campus stations (CAD 7), 45tours for the francophone ones (CAD 200). Your song is MAPL-Canadian, so every station needs it for its quota. Commercial radio in Montreal: low odds without a promoter — I'll say so rather than rank it.
> **Artist:** And Spotify editorial?
> **Agent:** The form closes 7 days before release, so you have four weeks. One song. Want the pitch text now?
