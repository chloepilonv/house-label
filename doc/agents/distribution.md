# Distribution

**Status:** 🧪 beta

> the desk that gets a finished record into the stores — and tells you what each route costs out of your own money.

## In a real label

**Who.** A major owns its distribution company; Berklee lists *Head of Digital Distribution* as the mid-to-senior post under the label's general manager ([Berklee](https://www.berklee.edu/careers/roles/label-manager)). An independent signs with a distributor — Sub Pop still lists "Direct Sales to Stores (US only)" as one of its seven front doors ([subpop.com/contact](https://www.subpop.com/contact)). A self-releasing artist uses a distributor directly, because Spotify, Apple Music and Amazon accept music only from approved providers ([Apple Music for Artists](https://artists.apple.com/support/1108-get-your-next-release-on-apple-music)).

**Week to week.** The desk delivers each release to the stores with its audio, artwork, codes and credits; sets release dates far enough out for the editorial pitch; handles the pitches only a distributor can make (Apple Music and Deezer take pitches from distributors and labels, not artists — [Apple Music Connect](https://musicconnect.apple.com/), [Deezer](https://creatorsupport.deezer.com/hc/en-us/articles/14963453830045-Pitching-Tool)); reads the monthly statements; and handles takedowns and transfers.

**What they produce.** Live releases on every store, on time, with the right identifiers — and the statement that pays the master royalties.

**What it costs.** A distributor's own published price, read 2026-09-08 unless noted:

| Model | Example | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Flat yearly fee, 0% of store revenue | DistroKid | $24.99 / $44.99 / $89.99 a year ([distrokid.com](https://distrokid.com/pricing/)) |
| Pay per release, then a cut forever | CD Baby | $9.99 a single + 9% of streaming ([cdbaby.com](https://cdbaby.com/cd-baby-cost/)) |
| Free, funded by a cut | RouteNote 15% · UnitedMasters 10% · Symphonic 30% | each company's pricing page, 2026-08-23 |
| Priced in CAD | Distribution Amplitude (Montréal, non-profit) | $40 to $649 a year by artist count ([distributionamplitude.com](https://distributionamplitude.com/en/nos-forfaits/)) |
| Negotiated (AWAL, The Orchard, Believe) | — | nobody publishes a rate; "reported at 15–30%" is a third party's account |

A fee and a cut are the same bill at one revenue: $99 a year equals 15% at about $660 of streaming income. And "keep 100%" means 100% of **one** pot — the recording royalty. Songwriting money (SOCAN, ASCAP/BMI, The MLC) and performer money (Re:Sound, SoundExchange) are collected elsewhere, by the rights desk.

**Where the line is.** Distribution delivers; it never presses vinyl (the plant does) and never issues your codes on its own authority (the rights desk explains who does). Which playlists to pitch and what to write is the promotion desk's; this desk only knows *who may* pitch each store and by when.

## Here

The Distribution Agent reads the distributor market for *you* — your country, your catalogue, your revenue — rather than in the abstract, and then gets one finished release ready to hand over. It compares the 39 distributors in our catalog on six dimensions with the real cost of each cut over your own numbers, says plainly what switching would cost, and walks you to the signup door with the documents in hand. You create the account. Open it with `houselabel distribution`.

| Skill | What you get | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Compare distributors | Twelve questions, then a shortlist of three to five ranked on your own two priorities, each with what it costs, what it keeps, and the one thing that would rule it out. The whole table on request. Your choice is saved so nobody re-argues it. | ✅ developed |
| Prepare the delivery package | Thirteen questions (fewer if it can read the specs off your files), then a per-release checklist — audio, artwork, metadata, identifiers, timing — each item ready / missing / unknown, each naming whose rule it is, each missing item with the fix. | ✅ developed |

**What it never does**

- Creates the account or uploads the release. The signup link is the last line; you click it.
- Rules on a contract. What exclusivity or a takedown clause *means* is knowledge; whether to sign is not its call.
- Issues an ISRC or a UPC. That question goes to the rights desk.
- Repeats a store count as coverage. "150+ stores" is a vendor's claim and is quoted with the vendor's name on it.
- Fills a gap with a guess. "They don't publish it" is an answer; an unknown ships with that company's help-centre link.

**What it needs from you**

On file already: your country and language, the releases you have entered with their distributor, your songs and their codes. It asks for: your yearly streaming revenue (a band is enough), the size of the catalogue that would have to move, your two priorities, and — for a package — the files or their specs, the release date, and the lyrics.

**Example**

> **Artist:** I'm on CD Baby for two singles. A friend says DistroKid is cheaper. Is it?
> **Agent:** Depends on one number: what did those singles earn on streaming last year, roughly?
> **Artist:** Maybe $300.
> **Agent:** Then CD Baby's 9% is $27 a year on top of what you already paid; DistroKid's cheapest plan is $24.99 a year flat. Nearly the same bill today — DistroKid pulls ahead as you release more. Switching re-uploads both singles with the same ISRCs; play counts usually carry over, and no distributor guarantees it. Want the full comparison?
